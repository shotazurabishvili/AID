# Lit Note: Deaton & Cartwright (2018)

**Full cite (APA 7):** Deaton, A., & Cartwright, N. (2018). Understanding and misunderstanding randomized controlled trials. *Social Science & Medicine*, 210, 2–21. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2017.12.005
**DOI / URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29331519/

## Argument
RCTs are over-interpreted across applied economics and the policy community. The paper argues that randomization does not by itself produce unbiased *average* treatment effects in the way standard practice assumes; covariate imbalance, heterogeneity, and the absence of a credible causal model can each undermine the routine "RCT = gold standard" framing. The methodological community routinely claims more than the RCT design itself supports.

## Method
- Conceptual / methodological essay
- Re-examination of the inferential warrants for RCT-derived ATEs
- Multiple worked examples from health and development

## Our engagement
**Path-c methodological-reflection peer citation.** Engaged at §1 (Introduction, ¶4) and §2 strand (c) Lit Review. The parallel for our paper is at the *over-claiming* level: just as Deaton-Cartwright argue the RCT community over-claims internal-validity warrants, we argue the cross-country-panel community over-claims robustness when measure-fragility and typology-irrecoverability haven't been disciplinedly tested. We cite Deaton-Cartwright as part of the lineage that named this kind of over-claim explicitly, even though the specific method critiqued differs from ours.

## Key claims to engage or cite
- Randomization does not by itself license the inferential moves the RCT community routinely makes
- The methodological community's interpretation of RCT findings is often weaker than the design supports
- Honest reporting of what an empirical method *can* and *cannot* claim is itself a methodological contribution
