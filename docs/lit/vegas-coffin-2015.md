# Lit Note: Vegas & Coffin (2015)

**Full cite (APA 7):** Vegas, E., & Coffin, C. (2015). When education expenditure matters: An empirical analysis of recent international data. *Comparative Education Review*, 59(2), 289–304.

## Argument
The relationship between **education spending and learning outcomes** is conditional. Below ~$8,000 per student in cumulative spending, more spending raises outcomes. Above that threshold, additional spending has no measurable effect on learning. The "spending → learning" elasticity is not constant.

## Method
- Cross-country panel using PISA scores
- Quadratic and threshold specifications on cumulative per-student spending
- Tests for diminishing returns

## Our engagement
**Directly relevant to our control structure and the spending threshold question.** Cited in §2 (Lit Review) and §5 (Discussion). Their conditional elasticity supports our argument that **input volumes matter less than how inputs are used** — which our Model 4 (intervention typology) operationalizes.

## Key claims to engage or cite
- Cumulative per-student spending has a threshold beyond which additional spending does not raise learning
- Most low- and middle-income countries are below this threshold (so spending could matter for them) but allocation efficiency still varies
- Effect heterogeneity by spending level matters for ODA allocation policy
