# Lit Note: Easterly, Levine & Roodman (2004)

**Full cite (APA 7):** Easterly, W., Levine, R., & Roodman, D. (2004). Aid, policies, and growth: Comment. *American Economic Review*, 94(3), 774–780.

## Argument
The Burnside-Dollar (2000) finding that aid only works in good-policy environments **does not survive an extended sample**. Their Aid × Policy interaction loses significance when more countries and years are added. The result is fragile to sample composition.

## Method
- Extended the original Burnside-Dollar (2000) sample
- Replicated their specification
- Tested alternative interaction specifications

## Our engagement
**Methodological warning.** Cited in §2 (Lit Review) and especially §6 (Discussion). The lesson for our paper: **interaction effects in cross-country panels are fragile**. We pre-commit to the panel-FE specification (Model 2) precisely because it does not depend on a particular interaction story. Robustness across samples (2000–2022 vs 2005–2020) and across the China-inclusive vs DAC-only sub-samples is required to claim our finding survives.

## Key claims to engage or cite
- Cross-country aid-effectiveness regressions are sensitive to sample
- Interaction-effect findings should be reported with multiple sample variants
- Be cautious of "aid works under condition X" stories
