# Lit Note: Vivalt (2020)

**Full cite (APA 7):** Vivalt, E. (2020). How much can we generalize from impact evaluations? *Journal of the European Economic Association*, 18(6), 3045–3089. https://doi.org/10.1093/jeea/jvaa019
**DOI / URL:** https://academic.oup.com/jeea/article/18/6/3045/5908781

## Argument
Empirically tests external validity in development RCTs. Meta-analysis of 635 impact evaluation studies across 20 development outcomes finds that effect sizes vary substantially across contexts — heterogeneity is large enough that pooled or single-RCT estimates often do not generalize. The implication is that the standard practice of treating an RCT result as a *transportable* effect estimate is overconfident; the conditions a result holds under should be specified, not assumed.

## Method
- Meta-analysis of impact-evaluation studies across 20 outcomes
- Heterogeneity decomposition (within- vs between-study)
- Bayesian hierarchical models for effect-size pooling
