# Lit Note: Sandefur (2018)

**Full cite (APA 7):** Sandefur, J. (2018). *Internationally comparable mathematics scores for fourteen African countries*. Center for Global Development Working Paper No. 444.

## Argument
**International learning comparisons across testing regimes are not as harmonized as they appear.** Anchor-based linking between PISA, TIMSS, SACMEQ, and other assessments produces score-equivalence claims that may not hold in practice — particularly for sub-Saharan African countries that anchor through small overlapping samples.

## Method
- Re-analysis of SACMEQ and PISA overlapping country items
- Estimation of item-level equating uncertainty
- Comparison of harmonized HLO-style rankings against single-assessment rankings

## Our engagement
**Critical for our HLO outcome variable defense.** Cited in §3 (Data & Methodology, specifically §3.4) and engaged head-on rather than buried. The Sandefur critique is our biggest threat on the outcome side — a referee can use it to say "your harmonized score is not really comparable."

Our defense in §3.4:
1. Acknowledge the critique openly
2. Note that **Model 2 (within-country fixed effects) does not require cross-country score comparability** — only within-country variation over time, which faces a much smaller harmonization burden
3. Report robustness with the AAP-2018 dataset (slightly different harmonization) and with single-assessment subsamples (PISA-only, TIMSS-only) where available

## Key claims to engage or cite
- Anchor sample sizes are often too small for credible item-equating
- Cross-test rankings are sensitive to harmonization assumptions
- The smoothing in HLO obscures real measurement uncertainty
- For SSA countries especially, harmonization rests on thin empirical foundations
