# Lit Note: Hanushek & Woessmann (2008, 2015)

**Full cite (APA 7):**
- Hanushek, E. A., & Woessmann, L. (2008). The role of cognitive skills in economic development. *Journal of Economic Literature*, 46(3), 607–668.
- Hanushek, E. A., & Woessmann, L. (2015). *The knowledge capital of nations: Education and the economics of growth*. MIT Press.

## Argument
Cognitive skills (measured by international assessments) — not years of schooling — are the link between education and economic growth. The book extends to a "knowledge capital" measure that aggregates test-score performance into a single country-level variable and shows it predicts growth far better than enrollment-based measures.

## Method
- Cross-country growth regressions
- Test-score harmonization across PISA/TIMSS/PIRLS cycles
- Instrumental variables for institutional quality

## Our engagement
**Foundational framework.** Cited in §1 (Introduction) and §2 (Literature Review). The brief frames our argument as the *education-aid-specific* extension of their thesis: if learning quality is what matters for development, and aid is supposed to support development, then aid that fails to move learning is structurally misaligned. We engage by extending the cognitive-skills logic from growth → aid effectiveness.

## Key claims to engage or cite
- Years of schooling ≠ cognitive skills; the gap is large and consequential
- Cognitive skills explain a substantial share of cross-country income variation
- Education systems vary widely in how much learning they produce per year of enrollment
