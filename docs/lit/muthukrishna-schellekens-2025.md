# Lit Note: Muthukrishna & Schellekens (2025)

**Full cite (APA 7):** Muthukrishna, M., & Schellekens, P. (2025, December). *The next great divergence: Why AI may widen inequality between countries*. United Nations Development Programme, Regional Bureau for Asia and the Pacific.
**DOI / URL (primary):** https://www.undp.org/asia-pacific/publications/next-great-divergence
**Secondary version:** Brookings Institution article (2026-01-08), https://www.brookings.edu/articles/next-great-divergence-how-ai-could-split-the-world/

## Argument
AI, like past general-purpose technologies (steam, electricity, computing), risks driving a new global divergence between countries that can absorb its productivity gains and those that cannot. The authors argue this divergence is not inevitable but requires deliberate action — without intervention, low-readiness countries fall further behind as AI-driven productivity asymmetries widen. The historical analogue (the Industrial Revolution dividing the world into industrialized and non-industrialized economies) is the cautionary frame.

## Method
- Conceptual / policy-analytical essay
- Cross-country positioning analysis using public indicators of AI readiness, human capital, and economic structure
- Historical-analogue argumentation (Industrial Revolution as the prior great divergence)
