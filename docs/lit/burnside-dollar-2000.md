# Lit Note: Burnside & Dollar (2000)

**Full cite (APA 7):** Burnside, C., & Dollar, D. (2000). Aid, policies, and growth. *American Economic Review*, 90(4), 847–868.

## Argument
Aid is effective in promoting growth, but **only in countries with good fiscal, monetary, and trade policies**. The policy environment moderates the aid–growth relationship.

## Method
- Cross-country panel growth regressions
- Aid × Policy interaction term
- Instrumental variables for aid

## Our engagement
**Aid effectiveness baseline.** Cited in §1 (Introduction) and §2 (Literature Review). Our paper is the education-specific version of this debate: where they ask "does aid plus good policy → growth," we ask "does aid plus what → learning." We extend their conditioning logic from policy to *structural determinants* (governance, conflict, prior expenditure).

The Easterly-Levine-Roodman (2004) refutation of Burnside-Dollar is also cited; we frame our paper as aware of the methodological lessons from that debate (sensitivity to sample, fragility of interaction effects).

## Key claims to engage or cite
- Aid × Policy interaction can be significant and positive in growth regressions
- Implication for aid allocation: target good-policy countries
