# Lit Note: Pritchett (2013)

**Full cite (APA 7):** Pritchett, L. (2013). *The rebirth of education: Schooling ain't learning*. Center for Global Development.

## Argument
Schooling expansion in developing countries has succeeded in getting children into classrooms but **failed to produce learning**. The global education system has been organized around inputs (enrollment, completion, expenditure) rather than outcomes (what children can actually do). Aid and policy frameworks reinforce this misalignment.

## Method
- Synthesis of cross-country evidence on schooling vs learning
- Conceptual framing of "isomorphic mimicry" — education systems adopt forms of effective schools without their substance
- Policy analysis of donor and government incentive structures

## Key claims to engage or cite
- Schooling ≠ learning; the gap is structural, not merely a data problem
- Aid and policy frameworks track inputs because outputs are politically inconvenient
- "Isomorphic mimicry" — institutions adopt the appearance of effectiveness without substance
- Donor incentive structures reward enrollment growth and completion rates
